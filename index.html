Abstract

Controlled edits across the bony roof angle ranges used in the Graf system could provide infant hip ultrasound examples for teaching angle measurement and evaluating measurement algorithms. The technical challenge is to convert an angle request into visible image changes while retaining the surrounding tissue appearance. We introduce a local editing method that separates analytical landmark control from image synthesis. An angle solver converts the requested bony roof angle α and cartilage roof angle β into target landmarks while preserving the baseline and roof segment lengths. Distance-based landmark and line-segment maps encode the target geometry. A conditional generator receives these maps, the source crop, and a pixelwise edit mask. The predicted content is blended locally with the source image. An optional step translates source femoral head texture into the generated region, with reduced texture blending near the requested lines. To assess the contribution of the landmark and line maps, we compared generators trained with and without them on the same previously analyzed ultrasound images. An automatic detector measured the angles in the edited images. On 612 requests to change the bony roof angle, target-angle mean absolute error was 3.320° with geometry maps and 9.843° without them. The method could support the development of infant hip ultrasound image sets with controlled bony roof and cartilage roof angle variations for teaching and evaluating angle-measurement methods.

Method

Our angle-controlled method uses a grayscale image, five source landmarks and requested bony roof and cartilage roof angles, α* and β*. A fixed 256 × 256 native-pixel crop is centered on the mean source position of p3, p4 and p5, with its origin shifted inside the image. The angle solver keeps p1, p2 and p3 fixed and rotates the roof segments to construct p4* and p5*. It preserves both source segment lengths and their directional branches.

Five point maps and three finite-line-segment maps encode the requested geometry. These eight maps, the source crop and a soft edit mask form the generator input. The mask covers source and target roof neighborhoods and includes source and target head neighborhoods when a head-diameter annotation H is available. We blend the prediction locally with the source and return the crop to its original position. Grayscale pixels outside the crop remain unchanged before overlays or format conversion.

Reconstruction uses the source crop as its reference; angle editing uses a thin-plate-spline reference constructed from the requested landmarks. Training combines pixel, VGG, adversarial, feature-matching, cycle-pixel and cycle-VGG losses. The matched generator comparison uses the same data, initialization and optimization conditions across five configurations; constructed references are not anatomical ground truth.

For optional head-texture translation, H defines source center f and radius. The baseline-parallel unit axis u points toward p4 relative to p3; its perpendicular v points toward f relative to p3. Both axes remain fixed at the source geometry. With source baseline length L and Δα = α* − α, the prescribed target is f* = f + Δα L (−0.0124 u − 0.0131 v). The coefficients describe pooled cross-sectional geometry, not a longitudinal developmental trajectory.

Nearest-neighbor backward sampling translates the original source texture to the prescribed head position. Blending weights decrease near the requested measurement lines. An optional second call to the same generator refines the target head boundary. These operations prescribe texture placement; they do not measure achieved anatomy. Their net benefit remains unresolved in the current evaluation.

The study uses a fixed three-line UNet++ detector to measure angles in edited images separately from the requested landmarks. The abstract's geometry-map comparison concerns matched generators trained with and without those maps. Full-method evaluation changes α while keeping β fixed; full-method β requests and simultaneous angle changes remain unassessed. Independent anatomical, clinical and educational validation is still needed.

This browser demonstration decodes three predicted Graf-line heatmaps into five proposed source points for review and confirmation; H is annotated manually. Direct landmark dragging and manual head targets are interface extensions, not separately evaluated manuscript results. Identity between the public ONNX models and the study evaluation versions has not been verified. The public resources demonstrate inference and interaction, not the complete training and evaluation pipelines. No clinical use is intended.
